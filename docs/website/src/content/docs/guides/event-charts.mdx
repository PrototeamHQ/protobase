Every meter of an energy company sends a reading a minute, so `metering.readings` grows by millions of rows a month. A chart of 90 days over those rows reads them all each time someone opens the dashboard. This guide gives the readings an hourly [time rollup](/reference/data-config/#time-rollups) and charts that instead: a few thousand rows, the same answers.

## 1. The events and their rollup

<Code code={timeRollups} lang="ts" title="config/metering/data.ts" />

`readings` is the table the meters write to. `readings_hourly` is a resource over it with one row per hour, organization and meter: how many readings fell in it (`events`), their total and highest watts (`watts_sum`, `watts_max`) and their total cost (`cost_sum`). Run [`protobase migration`](/reference/cli/#migration) and apply the file it writes: it creates the rollup's table and indexes and fills it.

## 2. A dashboard on the rollup

<Code code={timeRollupsPage} lang="tsx" title="config/metering/page.tsx" />

Each [`Chart`](/reference/blocks/#chart) names the rollup as its `resource` and groups by `bucket`, or by `meter_id`. A chart counts the readings the buckets hold, and its measure names the rollup's field: `average="watts_sum"` divides the watts by the readings, as an average over the readings themselves would. The days, weeks and months are the viewer's, for any time zone a whole number of hours off UTC.

## 3. What a rollup cannot answer

A rollup knows nothing finer than its hour, so a chart of 15 minutes, or of days that start at half past the hour, such as India's, is refused rather than answered wrongly. Point such a chart at `readings` instead. [Charts over a rollup](/reference/data-config/#charts-over-a-rollup) lists every measure and what is refused.
